# Collateral Sheet — the personalized sheet a salesperson writes up

## Why this exists

**A salesperson said "let me write something up for you."** That is the whole moment. Not a brochure,
not a campaign — one person on one call telling one other person they will put something together for
them to look at.

**What normally happens next is the problem.** That promise goes into a queue. It gets scoped,
drafted, reviewed, branded, corrected, approved. Sixteen hands touch it, and what finally arrives is a
generic brochure three weeks after the conversation that made it worth reading. The personalization —
the only reason it was going to work — is the first thing the process grinds off.

**So this skill does the first 90% and hands the person the last 10%.** It reads what was actually
said, resolves what has already been decided, writes both versions, proves them, registers them and
drafts the covering note. What is left for the human is the part that genuinely needs them: adjust a
line, attach it, send it. That is the same tenet the framework applies to every other job — the system
carries the administrative weight so a person spends their judgment where judgment is the point — and
this skill is an instance of it, not an exception to it.

**And it is what makes the sheet worth sending at all.** Because the artifact is produced from the
conversation instead of from a template library, it can be about the reader's own situation, in the
reader's own terms, aimed at the thing *they* want — which is the only reason anybody acts. That line,
from the reader's why through to the company's goal, is what the sheet is tracing.

**Two people are in every sheet, and every rule below serves one of them:**

- **The recipient**, who is standing in front of a decision they cannot make yet and should not have to
  become an expert to make. What lands in their inbox is the only evidence they have about whether we
  listened and whether we understand their business. A wrong claim about their world does not read as a
  small error — it reads as *they don't know us*, and that ends it.
- **The person who has to send it**, who is spending a relationship they already have. A sheet that is
  generic, wrong or late costs them something they cannot get back, and they often cannot tell before
  they hit send.

If a step below ever reads as ceremony, ask which of those two it protects. If the answer is neither,
the step is wrong — fix it here.

---

You are the engine. You write only the **content** (the sheet data). The **family's locked builder**
writes the finished file. Never hand-write markup, never hand-roll a layout, never rebuild the shell to
fix one line.

**This skill contains no rules of its own.** Every rule it applies is already ruled, already written
down, and already has exactly one home. What this skill owns is the **ORDER**, and one gate at the front
that makes a session resolve those homes instead of remembering them.

---

## GATE 0 — Resolve five things before you write a word

Do not start at step 1. Every one of these is a **blank field** the rest of the work will silently fill
with a guess — and a guess here does not error. It produces well-formed work built on a smaller world,
and the more disciplined the session, the more confidently wrong the output.

**Print all five resolutions before drafting.** Not because the operator needs them, but because a
resolution you have to print is one you cannot skip.

### 0.1 THE THREE WHYS — resolve all three, in this order, and keep them apart

**There are three different why-and-frame pairs in every sheet, and they get confused because they are
the same shape.** Name all three out loud before drafting. Getting them in the wrong order is how a
sheet ends up being about us.

**① OUR company's why and frame — the sender's.**
Resolve it from the company's own published profile: the reason this business exists, and its
**positioning** — the category it is in, its core claim, and what it is *against* in the field. This is
what makes the sheet ours rather than anyone's. It is a per-company fact and it is stored; do not
paraphrase it from the product.

**② THE RECIPIENT COMPANY's why and frame.**
What *their* business is trying to do, and the frame they see their own world through. This is the
layer the sheet is actually about.

> ⚠ **This one has no store, so it is TIERED and often absent — handle that, do not paper over it.**
> It arrives one of three ways, and you say which:
> **(a) asked for** — our own user requested it and it came back; **(b) said on the call** — the right
> person was there and described their business, so it is in the transcript; **(c) absent** — nobody
> has ever told us.
>
> In (a) and (b) it is **evidence, not a resolved fact**, and it inherits Step 1's rule exactly:
> anything you could not read, you mark on the page as an assumption and hand to the recipient to
> correct. In (c) you say it is absent and write ③ without inventing ② underneath it. **A recipient
> company's why that you inferred and did not mark is the most expensive sentence in the document.**

**③ THE RECIPIENT PERSON's why and frame — the human who opens it.**
Two halves, and both are needed:
- the **persona** half is stored: the asset's **program** carries `reader_job` (what the reader hires
  this asset to do), `audience` (who they are) and `signal` (the condition that summons it);
- the **person** half comes from the source — what *this* human actually said they were trying to get
  done, which is often narrower than the persona and occasionally contradicts it. **When they
  disagree, the person wins and you say so.**

> **② AND ③ NEST — ③ SITS INSIDE ② WHEN ② EXISTS.** This is the mirror image of our own tier stack:
> ours runs house → company → department → person; theirs runs **their organisation → their person**,
> read from the outside in. So when ② is present, ③ is written *inside* it — this person's job, in the
> context of what their business is trying to do. When ② is absent, ③ stands alone and the sheet says
> so rather than quietly inventing the outer ring.
>
> ⚠ **They are two different facts and they were blurred until 2026-08-20.** A company's goal and one
> executive's job are not the same thing, and a sheet aimed at the average of them lands with neither.

> ⭐ **WHY ALL THIS PERSONALIZATION IS THE MECHANISM AND NOT A FLOURISH — the operator, 2026-08-20:**
> *"No one kills their own baby."* If the recipient's organisation and the recipient themselves have
> told us what matters to them and why, and the sheet is built out of **their own words**, then what
> lands is partly theirs. People do not throw out their own thinking. That is the entire reason the
> three whys are resolved in this order and quoted rather than paraphrased — not thoroughness, and not
> politeness. It is what makes the document survive contact with the person who receives it.

**Then: our company's LENS drafts the message inside ② and ③.** The advisor sequence resolved at 0.3
is that lens — its top rails are a why and a frame, and they are ours. They are how we write. They are
**not** what the sheet is about. A sheet written inside *our* why instead of theirs is a brochure, and
the reader can tell in four seconds.

> ⭐ **THE ORDER IS NOT A PREFERENCE AND NOT A STYLE — IT IS THIS SKILL.** ① our why and frame goes in
> front, ② their company's why and frame is what the sheet is about, ③ the individual's why and frame is
> who it is written to, and our lens is what writes it. Ruled by the operator, 2026-08-20: *"it has to
> be that order for this skill no matter what... if you're not putting it in the why of the other
> person, but with our company why and frame in front of it, and then their company why and frame with
> our lens, I don't know how else it's gonna work."* Run them in that order or the sheet is a brochure.
> Everything below is machinery; this is the thing the machinery is for.

**REFUSES:**
- **running them out of order, or skipping one.** Three whys, that sequence, every sheet.
- stating any of the three from this body, a sibling file, a remembered sentence, or an inference from
  our own product. A why you supplied is a why nobody agreed to.
- collapsing ② and ③. A company's goal and one executive's job are different, and a sheet aimed at the
  average of them lands with neither.
- proceeding with **①** or the stored half of **③** missing. If the program has no `reader_job`, STOP
  and ask one plain question — *what is the reader trying to get done?* An unstated reader job is the
  fastest way to produce a beautiful sheet aimed at nobody.
- proceeding with **②** unstated. It may be *unproven* and marked; it may not be absent.

### 0.2 The governing spec — from the spec map, never from a sibling file

Resolve the spec map (tenant-scoped) for the slices this work touches. Two apply:

| Slice | What it governs here |
|---|---|
| `content-library-schema` | The asset row, the deliverable **filename**, and the FILE's **two homes** |
| `follow-through` | Whether a draft you leave behind can be sent by reflex |

Open the row the map returns and read the section it names. **Do not read this skill for the rule** —
the rule is in the spec, and this skill deliberately does not restate it.

For `follow-through` the map returns **two** rows, and that is correct rather than a two-homes defect:
the **canon** row owns the principle, the **standard** row carries its buildable detail. Canon for the
principle, the standard for the shape, never a third home.

**REFUSES:** a slice the map does not return, and a citation you have not opened. A pointer that
resolves is not a pointer that is true — a spec can name a real file that does not contain what your
sentence claims.

### 0.3 The advisor sequence — from the sequence store, BY CATEGORY

Resolve the sequences for the departments this asset serves, by category, exactly as `aii-advisors`
Step 1.5 does. Apply the advisors it returns, in the order it returns them. Honour every member's
`applies_when` condition — a conditional member that does not meet its condition is **excluded**, not
demoted.

**Read each member's `stage`, because rank is not the same as sequence.** Some members are *rails* — a
constraint on everything below them, never a step you pass and move on from. A session that treats a
top rail as step one, completes it and moves down the list has already lost the thing the order was
built to protect.

**This skill lists no advisors, on purpose.** A hand-typed roster inside a file is a copy of a queryable
store, and its staleness never surfaces as an error. It surfaces as disciplined work built on a smaller
world.

> **THE FAILURE THIS GATE EXISTS FOR, stated as a class rather than as somebody's incident.** A sequence
> gets taken from a header comment in a sibling content file instead of from the store. The store had
> been re-ordered days earlier. Every sheet built afterwards is correct in every visible way and built
> to a superseded order — and nothing catches it, because nothing was watching. It is caught, if at
> all, by a human happening to ask whether the advisors ran.

**REFUSES:** an empty sequence — route it to the adjudication gate, never fall back to a remembered
list — and any advisor you name that the store did not return.

**WRITES:** as each advisor is applied, record it — advisor, role, verdict, note — **at the moment of
use**, not at the end. Read the role and verdict vocabularies off the store rather than typing one, and
expect it to refuse a word it does not carry.

⚠ **The store records WHICH advisors, not in WHAT ORDER.** So a recorded pass proves you used live
advisors, not that you used the live order. Until that changes, **order is on you.**

### 0.4 The job — resolved, never invented

The asset's job carries the **guardrail** — the rule that keeps this piece on a through-line to the
goal — plus its owner and the default lens bench. The sheet restates none of it. It **obeys** it.

**REFUSES:** an unregistered job. It is refused and staged as a resolution gap. Do not invent one
inline, and do not pick the nearest-sounding one: two jobs are the same job only if they obey the same
guardrail.

### 0.5 The family, the template, the brand and the builder — from the instance layer

Resolve from the overlay and from a sibling asset row, never from this file:

- **which asset family** this is, and its declared `asset_type` — supplied by the Department Blueprint
  per department, so it is never a word written here
- the **template**, the **brand reference** and the **program** — take them off an existing sibling row
- the **locked builder** for that family: where it lives, how it is invoked, and **which assertions it
  owns**. Every family's builder enforces its own brand rules; they are that company's, not this
  skill's, and this skill must never name one.

**REFUSES:** starting from a sibling row whose brand reference or template is **NULL**. Null is not a
value — it means that row was written by a session that skipped this gate, and copying it propagates the
defect. Pick a populated sibling and board the null one.

## Step 1 — Read the source. Cite only what you actually opened.

Every claim traces to a real artifact: the transcript, the meeting recap, the CRM record, or the
operator's own notes. Name each source you read.

**REFUSES:** citing a recap, thread or transcript you did not open. The failure mode is specific and it
has happened: a source is named in good faith, never opened, and the sheet is one question away from
being reported as fabricated.

**Missing inputs do not block.** Build with the assumption **marked on the page** and surface it to the
recipient — they are the cheapest and most accurate corrector available. An unmarked assumption is the
defect; a marked one is the design.

## Step 2 — Write the canonical version FIRST, then the personalized one

Two files, in this order, and the order is load-bearing:

1. **Canonical** — written for the *audience* the program names, not for the account. Nothing
   account-specific survives except by deliberate choice. Provenance: original.
2. **Personalized instance** — inherits from the canonical, and names it as its source.

Where each FILE lives is ruled in the spec resolved at 0.2. Read it there.

> The guardrail on this family of work says it out loud: **it carries our name and works for the next
> one too.** A sheet that only works for one account has failed its job before anyone reads it.

**If you write only the personalized file, the canonical never exists and the next one starts from
zero.** That is not hypothetical — the inheritance half of this model is the part most often left
empty, and an empty inheritance field is machinery that exists and is unused.

## Step 3 — Say who owns the human decision, per item

**This is the step that makes the rest believable, and it is a framework tenet, not a flourish.** The
system carries the administrative weight; a person keeps the judgment. A sheet that hides where the
person still decides is selling a machine that replaces them, which is both untrue and unbuyable.

Two distinct shapes, and the sheet must say which one applies to **each** item:

- **GUARDRAILED CHOICE** — the system narrows to the two or three responses policy allows and marks one
  recommended; a qualified person picks. *That judgment IS the job.*
- **ESCALATION** — anything outside those options, and **any change to a rule itself**, goes to a
  **named person.** Never "whoever is on shift."

The distinction is not cosmetic in a regulated setting, where the rules differ by jurisdiction and
implying a shift worker owns a ruling is a real exposure. If the family's template supports a per-item
label, use it. If it does not, say so plainly rather than leaving the question open.

## Step 4 — Build on the family's locked builder

You write the content. The builder writes the file. It owns the type, the palette, the brand
assertions and the layout probes; content is data passed in.

**Where it runs.** Resolve it. A builder that renders through a headless browser needs one to be
present, and the runtime that has one is not always the runtime you are in. If you are on a surface
that cannot run it, **say so and stop** — do not hand-roll the output.

**REFUSES:** hand-writing markup, rebuilding the shell to fix one element, or editing the builder to
special-case one sheet. Patch, never rebuild — the existing design is ground truth.

## Step 5 — Prove it before you call it built

Not a claim. A run, with its output shown:

- every assertion the builder owns passes — **read them off the builder, do not type a list here**
- no layout overflow on any page
- no banned vocabulary
- **no unsourced claim** — walk the sheet against your Step 1 source list, line by line
- the output filename matches the shape the spec ruled at 0.2

## Step 6 — Register BOTH rows at creation

Both files. At creation, not later. Populated, not null: the brand reference, the template, the
program, the job, what success *is*, the provenance and its source, and the openable pointer to the
file.

The row's identity and the file's name are **two different facts** and follow two different rules — the
spec at 0.2 says so explicitly. Do not collapse them.

Name what success *is*, in a sentence, at registration. A variable not captured now cannot be
reconstructed later.

**REFUSES:** writing NULL where a value existed.

## Step 7 — Draft the covering note under the SYSTEM NOTE rule

The block, the empty recipient bar, and what it must say are ruled in the `follow-through` slice
resolved at 0.2. Read it there and follow it exactly. The load-bearing part is that **recipients come
out of the send bar**: every other element is advisory, that one makes the accident impossible.

This is also where the last 10% is handed over cleanly. The person's remaining job — check it, attach
it, adjust a line, send it — should be stated in the note in their terms, not left implied.

## The two doors — one body

**Door A — it shows up on its own.** A debrief lands carrying an action item that owes the other side a
document, and the sheet is underway before anyone asks.

**Door A is split in two, and the split is a capability fact rather than a compromise.** The sweep that
NOTICES runs in the client's own scheduled fleet. If that runtime cannot reach their files, brand
assets and a renderer, it **RAISES** the owed sheet as a named flag and stops. This skill **BUILDS** it,
wherever those are reachable.

> ⚠ **A sweep that reported a sheet as built would be fabricating a file that does not exist** — the
> exact failure this skill was written to prevent, committed by the thing meant to prevent it. That
> refusal belongs in the sweep's own contract as an acceptance test with a negative half.
>
> Per `aii-schedule-job`, **Door A is not called live until one real run has proven it.** Until then it
> is built, unwired and unfalsified — which is not the same as verified.

**Door B — in session.** "Build a sheet for X." Same body, same gate, same proof.

Neither door skips Gate 0. An unattended sweep is the *most* likely place for a stale resolution to go
unnoticed, because nobody is there to ask.

## What goes RED when this skill is skipped

A rule that is already correct, already published and already read, and gets broken anyway, is missing a
**test** — not a wording. These are the tests. Resolve the registered check for this tenant and run it;
its branches are:

| Branch | Goes red when | Catches |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | an asset row of this family is missing its brand reference, template or success metric | Step 6 skipped |
| **B** | an asset row carries no owner advisor, or no advisor rows at all | Gate 0.3 skipped: nobody recorded that a lens was ever run |
| **C** | a recorded advisor sits in no live sequence **and** no job bench | an invented or retired name |
| **D** | *(spoken, cannot red)* how much of the canonical→personalized model is actually in use | Step 2 never being reached |
| **E** | *(control)* the detector misses a planted defect — **or keeps firing after that same row is repaired** | the check itself going blind |

**Run it before you build and again before you call it done. Read E first** — if the detector has gone
blind, nothing above it means anything.

A backlog of reds is a finding, not a bug in the check. **Do not clear a red by narrowing the check** —
a check's own scoping boundary is the cheapest thing in the building to weaken.

> ⚠ **Two things this check cannot see, and both belong in its own honest limits rather than papered
> over.** **(1) ORDER** — if the store records which advisors but not their rank, branch B proves
> somebody was recorded and nothing more. **(2) A DELIVERED FILE THAT WAS NEVER REGISTERED** — that
> evidence is on a filesystem and a SQL check reads a database. A green check is not a clean sheet; it
> is three questions answered out of five.

## What this skill does NOT close

**The loop has no observe leg, and this skill must say so rather than imply one that closes.** The
measurement store the content-library spec depends on is **unbuilt**. What success *is* is required at
registration so the engine can name it. **Nothing yet reads whether it moved.** Register honestly, and
do not describe this as a loop that learns.

a defect in this file.*
