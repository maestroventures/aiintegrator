# Research

## Why this exists

**Every business deals with other businesses at every step of its work, and each of those dealings
goes better when you know who you are dealing with before you act.** The experience models name who
is on the other side, and each one is a reason to research:

| Who is on the other side | Why you research them | For example |
|---|---|---|
| A **prospect**, before you reach out (Demand Generation) | So the first message is about them: what changed for them this year, and why now | A new owner, new locations, a new filing |
| A **prospect**, before a call or a quote (Appointments, Project Sales) | So you know their business, and what they would do instead of buying from you | What they run on today; who they buy from now |
| **Who to go after at all** | So you choose the kinds of business that fit, how many exist, and what is urgent for them | Which industries have the problem you solve |
| A **customer**, at onboarding, renewal or when they leave (CS models, Memberships) | So you know what has changed for them, and what they run on | They opened locations, changed systems, merged |
| A **partner or reseller** | So you know they are real, who they serve, and whether they fit your customers | An agency that could resell you; a software maker to integrate with |
| A **vendor or supplier**, before you buy | So you know it is stable, who else uses it, and what it costs elsewhere | A software tool, a contractor, a supplier |
| An **investor or advisor**, before you ask (Investor & Advisor Relations) | So the ask fits what they back and how they work | Their past investments, their focus |
| **Competitors and the market** | So you know who else serves your customers, where they win, and where you lose | The alternative a customer falls back on when they do nothing |
| **Employers you hire against** (Hiring) | So an offer competes with what similar companies give | What comparable companies pay and offer |

**This skill researches businesses and kinds of business, never private individuals.** A person's
business role is part of their company's record. Their private life is no one's research.

When these facts are wrong or unsourced, everything built on them inherits the error. The message
quotes a wrong number, the call guide guesses at a pain, the offer misreads the market, the vendor
turns out not to be what it said.

**What it prevents.** Each of these happened at the first company to run on this framework:
- **Doing the same research twice,** because the first answer sat in a file nobody could find.
- **A fact nobody can check:** a number with no source, or a label we chose for a company instead of
  what it calls itself.
- **A confident wrong answer.** A table split across two pages was read as "no data," and 39
  franchise filings showed no location count. One that was checked by hand said 450.

**What the person gets:**
- an answer they can trust without re-checking it, because every claim shows where it came from and
  how sure it is;
- research done once and used by every company that inherits it;
- an honest list of what is still unknown.

**It is the "same way" in the owner's own loop:** same way is how you know what's working, and
what's working is what you make better. Research done the same way every time can be measured and
improved. Research done differently each time cannot.

**Stage:** 4, Do (`blueprint-skills.md` §9).

## Step 1 — Name the subject and the question

One subject per run: one company, or one kind of business. Write in one line the question the
research must answer, and who will use the answer. If you can't, clarify first (`aii-clarify`).

## Step 2 — Look in the store first

The subject may already be known. Search the store before searching the web, and research only
what is missing. A match is updated, never duplicated: **Resolve, never type** (`blueprint-core.md`
§5.17).

## Step 2.5 — Find the research tools this company has, from its registry

**Why:** a session that only knows its own web search misses what the company already pays for: a
company database, a people-finder, a social network, a deep-research service. And one that names a
tool from memory breaks the day that tool changes. *(Added 2026-10-29.)*

Ask the registry, BY CATEGORY (`blueprint-core.md` §11 rule 4), never by a tool's name, which
connectors answer `CAN` for what this question needs:
- **`web-research`** — search the web, read a page, research a company or person in depth;
- **`prospecting-data`** — find or enrich a company, find people by role, find a work email, match a
  named person, get a social profile.

Use every tool that answers `CAN` for the question, and the session's own web search and page reads
as the floor when none does. **A tool that spends money or credits is used only with the person's
yes**, and only for what the question needs. A tool registered with what it is strong and weak at
carries those notes: read them before you trust its answer (for example, a person found by name
alone needs a company match before it is believed).

**Write a coverage line** with the answer: each source and tool you tried, and whether it was read,
could not be read, or found nothing. A zero is a finding, and a source you could not read is named,
never guessed around.

**For a partner or reseller, add the partner view:** what they sell under their own name, and what
they supply under someone else's. It decides whether an offer could go out under their brand.

## Step 3 — Rank the sources, and record the rank

From most to least trusted:
1. The subject's own regulated filings (for example, a franchise disclosure document).
2. Its customers' own words: reviews, case studies, forums.
3. Its own website and published material.
4. Third-party write-ups.
5. Marketing claims about it.

Every fact keeps the rank it came from. Customers' words outrank the subject's description of itself.

## Step 4 — Gather what the question needs

**For a company:**
- what it does, in its own words, and who it serves (the word it uses for its customers);
- its lines of business, and the tools it runs on;
- its size, with how that was worked out;
- changes in the last year that make now a different moment (a new leader, funding, hiring, new
  locations, a new filing), each with its date;
- what it calls itself legally, and the evidence;
- the alternative its customers fall back on when they do nothing, and the named competitors,
  including honestly where they beat us.

**For a kind of business:**
- its lines;
- the software that serves each line, with the maker;
- the companies in it;
- the numbers that make its problem urgent;
- the changes that show a company in it is ready to buy.

## Step 5 — Every claim carries its evidence

The rule lives in the framework, not here: follow `blueprint-core.md` §5.17, "What you write is
clean, complete, and self-contained." For research, that means:
- a short quote, the link and the date it was read;
- the verified, inferred or unknown label on every claim.

Never choose a label for the subject. Record what it calls itself, plus the evidence, and leave the
judgement to the person.

## Step 6 — Read pages and documents without guessing

- Prefer a data feed or an export over the web page.
- Read tables with their layout kept, joined across page breaks.
- A page with no text layer, such as a scan, is reported as unread, never guessed.
- If a site's rules say not to collect, stop and ask.
- Never work around a CAPTCHA, a login or a paywall.
- Remove duplicates from what you collect before writing anything.

## Step 7 — Write to the store, never to a file

Each fact goes through its domain's own door: company, line of business, tool, signal. The framework
holds the kinds and the rules; the company's store holds the data. If no door exists for a fact,
stop: record the missing door as a commitment for the system, and don't park the fact in a file to come back to.

## Step 8 — Prove it and hand over

Read the written rows back (`aii-prove-it`). Then report, in plain words:
- what is now known, and where it lives;
- what is inferred, and why;
- what could not be found, and why.

The unknowns are part of the answer.

---

## When NOT to use

The company's own records (that is `aii-tune-up` and `aii-onboard-client`), and a single quick
lookup where nothing is stored.
